Trust, worked out from the evidence.

Anyone can contribute to Aknopla. Our technology decides how much weight each contribution deserves, and keeps every value current as the evidence changes.

Open to everyone, weighed one by one

We do not approve contributors in advance. Each contribution is weighed when it arrives, on the evidence it carries.

This is the research problem known as truth discovery: finding what is true when many sources disagree and none is known to be reliable beforehand. Our approach to it is built on data provenance.

From a contribution to a published value

  1. 1

    Contribute. Someone who visits or runs a place answers a set question about it, such as its time limit or its price.

  2. 2

    Record. The contribution is stored with its provenance: when, where and how it was made.

  3. 3

    Weigh. Its reliability is inferred from that record, from any supporting evidence such as a photo taken at the place, and from the contributor's track record.

  4. 4

    Estimate. Every contribution on the same question is combined into one current value, which is revised whenever new evidence arrives.

What makes the result reliable

Provenance over identity

Weight comes from a contribution's record, whoever the contributor says they are.

Independent sources

Agreement counts only when it comes from independent sources. Many accounts repeating one answer do not outweigh a few independent witnesses, so adding accounts adds no influence.

Track record

A contributor's reliability is learned from their history, including people who contribute only a few times.

Values that move

Every value is an estimate with a date. Recent evidence from the place itself can replace a long-standing answer.

Structured from the start

Each kind of place has its own schema: a fixed set of questions asked of every place of that kind. Contributions arrive as comparable data points, with no free text to interpret.

Two independent kinds of source

Visitors contribute through I Know A Place. Owners and staff contribute through Lantern-Rise. Both answer the same questions, which gives every place two independent kinds of source.
